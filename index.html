MacREU · Separation Processes

Direct Lithium Extraction from Synthetic Geothermal Brine

Tested aluminum hydroxide as a lithium host, focusing on whether lithium could be retained while competing ions were separated from a synthetic geothermal brine.

ProblemDistinguish lithium retained in the host structure from competing ions removed by precipitation or surface adsorption.
MethodPrepared synthetic brine, adjusted pH to control iron precipitation, and varied host loading and temperature. Heated the mixtures, redispersed them in water, and centrifuged to separate solids from the supernatant. Prepared labeled samples for multi-element ICP-OES analysis.
ResultThe initial host-loading screen gave similar responses, so two conditions were carried into dual-salt selectivity tests. Water washing and ICP-OES were the next steps to distinguish removable Na and K from lithium retained in the Li–Al layered-double-hydroxide host.

MacREU · Energy Materials

Hard-Carbon Anodes for Sodium-Ion Batteries

Investigated how hard-carbon particle morphology and structure affect sodium storage, comparing flake-like commercial powders with spherical MSE carbon.

ProblemSeparate limitations associated with carbon particle structure from those introduced during electrode fabrication.
MethodCompared SEM morphology and Raman/XRD structure with charge–discharge behavior. Varied binder, loading, and electrode thickness to test whether fabrication changes could overcome the flake-carbon capacity limit.
FindingFlake electrodes remained near 0.5 mAh despite fabrication changes, with storage dominated by surface interactions. The poster’s spherical MSE results showed more stable cycling, making particle morphology a priority for further anode development.

Project 01 · CSULB Beach Launch Team

Theseus Liquid-Propellant Rocket Recovery System

Modified Theseus's dual-drogue, single-main parachute recovery system and redesigned the avionics bay to increase the main parachute diameter from 4 to 6 inches through CAD modeling, fabrication, and assembly.

  • Supported a three-day field deployment and launch at Friends of Amateur Rocketry.
  • When main-chute deployment fractured a structural weak point, oversized safety margins in the dual drogues recovered approximately 85% of the rocket intact.
  • Promoted to GNC/Recovery Co-lead to design a new recovery system from the ground up, replacing a legacy architecture adapted for more than six years.

Research focus 01

Sodium-Ion Battery Anodes

Compared flake-like and spherical commercial hard carbons to connect particle shape, physical structure, and sodium-storage behavior for future anode development.

  • Applied SEM, TEM, Raman spectroscopy, and XRD for materials characterization.
  • Analyzed cycling behavior and theoretical capacity under practical charge/discharge conditions.
  • Found spherical hard carbon showed stronger capacity retention, faster diffusion, and greater stability than flake-like samples.

Research focus 02

Direct Lithium Extraction

Led experiments using aluminum hydroxide as a host for lithium uptake from synthetic geothermal brine, with the goal of developing scalable domestic lithium-recovery methods.

  • Screened reaction mass ratios and temperatures to evaluate extraction efficiency.
  • Tested selectivity against competing ions and prepared samples for ICP-OES analysis.
  • Investigated lithium recovery through layered-double-hydroxide conversion and water rinsing.
